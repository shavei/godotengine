# Pass It On: Research Notes

Research done on 2026-09-27 while choosing and shaping the concept. Web searches cannot prove that no small jam game has tried an idea; they show whether an idea is an established genre or a known game.

## 1. Originality check for the core idea

**Idea:** the hero permanently gives earned powers to villagers, which builds up the village (and later returns Techniques to the hero).

| Closest existing work | What it does | Difference |
|---|---|---|
| Kingdom Two Crowns | Monarch cannot fight; coins direct citizens | Weak from the start; no giving of the hero's own powers |
| Moonlighter | Dungeon loot sold, money funds new town merchants | Town grows from money, not from the hero's abilities |
| Idle RPG: Agent of Adventure | Teach adventurers skills | Teaching costs the player nothing |
| "Training the Peaceful Villagers" trope (Seven Samurai, Mount & Blade) | Hero trains villagers to fight | Hero does not lose the power |
| Apostasy, The Backwards RPG, Regression | Choose which abilities to lose | Loss is a puzzle, not an investment in others |
| Xenoblade Chronicles 2, Fire Emblem Engage | Living weapons, bonded allies | Not a village-building loop |

Searches for "hero transfers powers to townspeople", "give away abilities to villagers to upgrade town", "teach your skills to villagers" found no match for the full idea.

## 2. Design lessons applied

### 2.1 Loss aversion and hoarding
Players value what they own more than equal gains (endowment effect) and hoard options they will never use (Fallout container hoarding).
**Applied:** 3-slot cap forces gifts; the gift is framed as a transfer that is visible and celebrated; gifts return value later (Techniques); raids never destroy gifts.
- https://bumblingthroughdungeons.com/endowment-theory-in-games/
- https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/analysis-option-availability-and-loss-aversion-in-game-design
- https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262043533/achievement-relocked/
- https://hci.usask.ca/wp-content/cache/mendeley-file-cache/6eb59aeb-38be-37ad-950c-7c625cb4cce2.pdf

### 2.2 Dual loops drive "one more run"
Moonlighter's dungeon and town loops feed each other: loot funds the town, the town improves runs.
**Applied:** runs produce powers and materials; the village turns them into services and Techniques that improve runs.
- https://www.fandom.com/articles/moonlighter-addictive-dungeon-crawling-town-builder

### 2.3 Synergy discovery (Hades Duo Boons)
Combining two gods' boons into a unique Duo Boon is a core source of run variety and excitement in Hades.
**Applied:** 28 Fusions from pairs of kept powers; the same pairs reused as village Neighbor bonuses.
- https://hades.fandom.com/wiki/Duo_Boons
- https://www.cbr.com/hades-duo-boon-guide/

### 2.4 Depth vs complexity
Complexity is the number of moving parts; depth is how many meaningful options exist at a moment. Simple mechanics that interact create depth.
**Applied:** every system fits one sentence (GDD 1.3); depth comes from timing (keep now vs Master later), placement (neighbors) and pairing (fusions).
- https://game-wisdom.com/critical/depth-game-design
- https://www.accidentalcyclops.com/depth-vs-complexity/
- https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/depth-from-complexity-pt-2

### 2.5 Long-term difficulty (Slay the Spire Ascension)
Stacking difficulty levels, each adding one rule, are a major driver of long-term replay.
**Applied:** 10 Seasons, each with one twist, some targeting the Choice itself (Season 6: only 2 slots).
- https://slay-the-spire.fandom.com/wiki/Ascension
- https://rogueliker.com/slay-the-spire-review/

### 2.6 Defending what you built
Roguelite defense games (Bad North, The Rabbit Haul, City Defense Z) make a place matter by threatening it.
**Applied:** raids every 4 runs where gifts visibly defend the village.
- https://www.pcgamer.com/games/roguelike/defend-your-farm-from-a-gaze-of-raccoons-in-this-charming-tower-defense-roguelite-out-tomorrow/
- https://towerward.com/blog/best-roguelite-tower-defense-games

## 4. Runner-up original ideas (kept for the future)
- **Double or Nothing:** before each room, bet on your own performance (no hit, under 15 s). Closest: Hades Pact of Punishment, We Who Are About To Die, gambling roguelikes (CloverPit, Balatro).
- **Forget Me:** memories are currency; spending one erases a map region, an NPC's memory of you, or a recipe. Closest: Revita (health as currency), Mio: Memories in Orbit.
